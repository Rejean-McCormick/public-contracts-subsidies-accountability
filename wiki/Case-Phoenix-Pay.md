# Phoenix Federal Pay System

**Category:** Digital procurement  
**Evidence grade:** A  
**Geography:** Quebec / Canada  
**Research edition:** November 12, 2026

## 1. Background and scope
Payroll-system failure, remediation, public-service disruption and the distinction between normal administration and incremental failure cost.

## 2. Financial record and interpretation
Federal 2024–25 reporting identifies approximately CAD 937.5 million in Phoenix-related expenditure, including regular payroll operations. The full amount cannot be called avoidable waste.

**Accounting safeguard:** An initial budget, contract ceiling, authorized subsidy, cash disbursement, impairment and final economic loss are distinct measures. This page does not aggregate them into a single headline. Any figure must retain its source, currency, entity and reporting period.

## 3. Initial-price efficiency and public value
The investigation must establish what the public institution originally intended to buy or finance, the requirements known at approval, and the available alternatives. For procurement, estimate an equivalent efficient price using bottom-up costing, comparable bids and lifecycle obligations. For industrial finance, evaluate the risk-adjusted terms and public participation in future value. For executive pay, examine mandate, total remuneration, performance and independent oversight.

## 4. Governance, beneficiaries and contractor chain
Identify decision-makers, the authorizing body, procurement or investment advisers, legal recipients, prime suppliers, intermediaries, subcontractors and eventual public assets. A contract payment is not proof of net profit; a business relationship is not proof of conflict or misconduct. Record warnings and responses chronologically.

## 5. Priority documents and investigative steps
Reconstruct readiness decisions, testing, defects, remediation costs, employee impacts, replacement programs and the baseline cost of reliable payroll.

Seek original contracts and amendments, accepted deliverables, dated financial records, oversight reports, relevant court decisions, recoveries and responses from affected parties. Where documentation is unavailable, the proper conclusion is that the question remains open.

## 6. Questions still open
1. Was the original price or valuation independently tested?
2. Which amendments were caused by new requirements and which by avoidable failures?
3. How much cash was actually paid, and to which legal entities?
4. Which services, assets, intellectual property or public benefits remain?
5. What was impaired, repaid, recovered or sold?
6. Were procurement, governance and executive expense rules followed?
7. What official finding, if any, establishes misconduct?

## 8. Evidence and fairness
Grade A describes the state of documentation, not a judgment about a person or organization. Interpretations are provisional. No criminal conclusion or realized-loss estimate is inferred from the mere existence of a project, contract, public investment or executive compensation.
